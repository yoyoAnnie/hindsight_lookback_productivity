# 01 — Vision and Concept

## The problem

Creative work is scattered by default. One idea for one project can live as:

- a file or report in a drive,
- feedback in a chat thread,
- a daily note in a notes app,
- a sketch on paper, photographed and forgotten,
- a goal in the person's head, never seen in concrete form.

Switching between projects, spaces, and apps is the pain — not a lack of
tools. People lose ideas **between** tools.

## The dilemma underneath it

More information was supposed to make creation faster — and with AI tools,
inspiration is genuinely easier to get. Interviewees for this project
reported the **opposite** in practice: overload slowed them down. When
options multiply, narrowing is the hard part; people over-collect
references they never return to, and keep exploring new ideas instead of
working the ones they saved — moving further from their goal the more
they gather. This is classic information-foraging behaviour (weak scent
on saved material, zero cost to switch to a fresh patch), and it is why
Hindsight contains **no discovery feed at all** and rewards only actions
taken, never items collected. The full counter-design, including the
Garden, is in `11-garden-and-anti-foraging.md`.

## The idea

**Hindsight** is an ideas and brainstorm scrapbook journal: one continuous
stream where a person keeps, directs, and sees their thinking.

Instead of organising by folder or by app, Hindsight organises by *what you
are doing with the thought*:

- **Hand — Projects (Hold):** the material itself. Files, scripts,
  transcripts, podcast audio notes, reports, reference images.
- **Brain — Notes (Direct):** the person's daily instructions on that
  material — takeaways, comments, feedback (their own or others'), and the
  one change they want made today.
- **Eye — Visualize (See):** the same material rendered concretely — a mind
  map for an idea, a prototype for a product, a composition view for an
  artwork, a concept map for study — so the person can judge whether the
  idea is rich enough, clear enough, or interesting enough.

The name is the promise: because everything stays on one timeline, looking
back is useful. Hindsight is what the stream gives you.

## Who it is for first

People who think across formats and over days, not in one sitting:

- creators developing content (video, podcast, writing) from source material;
- students turning dense source material into understanding over weeks;
- makers/artists developing a piece through versions.

(Expansion beyond these three is a later decision, not an MVP assumption.)

## What makes it different

1. **One object, not three apps.** The file, the notes on it, and its
   visualisation are views and actions on a single *Work* — nothing is
   copied between spaces.
2. **A daily loop, not a storage bin.** The product is built around a small
   daily act (one takeaway, one tweak), not around uploading and forgetting.
3. **Seeing is a test.** Visualisation is not decoration; the Eye reports
   back what is thin, disconnected, or contradictory.
4. **Human touch.** Scrapbook cues — dates you can read, margin notes, kept
   marks — over a sterile dashboard feel. Plain human language throughout.

## What it is not

- Not a general notes app, not a file drive, not a project-management board.
- Not an AI that thinks for the user. The Brain is the *user's* judgement;
  any automation serves the loop (splitting, linking, rendering, flagging)
  and never replaces the daily direction.
- Not social in the MVP. Private by default (see `08-privacy-and-ip.md`).

## Style in one line

Futuristic Renaissance × Notion-clean: old-painting warmth and collage,
product clarity and restraint, seamless scroll-driven motion as the taste
signal. Full values in `05-design-system.md`.

## Success, in plain terms

A user opens Hindsight daily, works one Work through the loop, and can point
to a mind map or prototype and say whether the idea got better this week.
