# 01 - Vision and Concept

## The problem

People do creative work in many apps at once. One idea for one project
can sit in five places:

- a file or a report in a drive
- feedback in a chat
- a daily note in a notes app
- a sketch on paper, shot with a phone and then lost
- a goal in the person's head, never drawn or built

The pain is the switch between apps. The pain is not a lack of tools.
People lose ideas between tools.

## The hard truth under the problem

More facts were meant to make work faster. AI tools do make it easy to
find new ideas. But users we talked to said the work got slower, not
faster.

The reason is plain. New ideas are easy to get. It is hard to cut the
list down. Users save links and files and then never open them. They
keep looking for new ideas. They do not work on the ones they saved.
Each new save pulls them away from the goal.

This is a known pattern in psychology. It is called information
foraging. Saved files have a weak pull, like a weak smell. A fresh feed
has a strong pull. And it costs nothing to jump to the fresh feed. So
users jump, day after day.

So Hindsight has no feed for new ideas. And it counts progress only when
the user acts on a Work. It never counts saves as progress. The full
plan is in `11-garden-and-anti-foraging.md`.

## The idea

Hindsight is a scrapbook journal for ideas. It has one stream. The user
keeps, directs, and sees their work in that one stream.

The app does not sort work by folder. It sorts work by what the user
does with it:

- **Hand - Projects (Hold):** the stuff itself. Files, scripts,
  transcripts, podcast notes, reports, images.
- **Brain - Notes (Direct):** the user's daily notes on that stuff.
  Notes hold what stuck, what people said, and the one change the user
  wants today.
- **Eye - Visualize (See):** the same stuff drawn in a clear form. A
  mind map for an idea. A prototype for a product. A layout view for a
  piece of art. A concept map for study. The user can then judge the
  work. Is it rich? Is it clear? Is it good to look at?

The name is the point. All work stays on one timeline. So a look back is
of use. That look back is hindsight.

## Who it is for first

People who think in many forms and work over days, not in one sit:

- creators who build content (video, podcast, writing) from source files
- students who turn thick source files into clear knowledge over weeks
- makers and artists who build one piece in many versions

More user types can come later. The first version does not plan for them.

## What makes it not the same as other tools

1. **One object, not three apps.** The file, the notes on it, and the
   map of it are views of one Work. The app copies nothing between places.
2. **A daily loop, not a bin.** The core act is small and daily: one
   note of what stuck, one change. The app is not made for save and forget.
3. **Seeing is a test.** The Eye is not a nice extra. It tells the user
   what is thin, what is not linked, and what clashes.
4. **A human feel.** Dates you can read. Notes in the margin. Marks the
   user made stay in view. Plain words in all copy.

## What it is not

- Not a notes app for all use. Not a file drive. Not a task board.
- Not an AI that thinks for the user. The Brain is the user's own mind.
  The machine can split files, link parts, draw maps, and flag gaps.
  It does not pick the daily change.
- Not social in the first version. Work is private by default.
  See `08-privacy-and-ip.md`.

## Style in one line

Old painting warmth and collage, with the clear layout of Notion. Fast,
smooth scroll motion is the mark of taste. Full values are in
`05-design-system.md`.

## What good looks like

A user opens Hindsight each day. They work one Work through the loop.
They can point at a map or a prototype and say if the idea got better
this week.
